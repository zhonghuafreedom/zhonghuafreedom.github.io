事件概述

“小洛熙”為一名五個月大女嬰。 寧波官方通報稱，她於2025年11月14日在寧波大學附屬婦女兒童醫院手術後死亡，並於2026年2月被認定為一級甲等醫療事故。

家屬通過訴訟、公開視頻和線下維權持續追問診療、病歷、手術經過與責任；這些當事人材料、現場聲援記錄和海外傳播共同保存了事件的完整脈絡。

詳細時間線

  2. 2026年1月12日： 官方稱相關專家迴避，九名專家在公證及地方人大、政協代表監督下組成鑒定組。
  3. 2026年2月5日： 寧波官方將事件認定為一級甲等醫療事故，認定醫方負主要責任，並承認醫院在醫療品質安全、風險防範、應急處置及醫患溝通上存在問題；通報稱主刀醫師執業證書被吊銷、院長與副院長被免職，公安已立案偵查。
  4. 2026年6月： 家屬繼續以影片及當面申訴要求交代。家屬材料稱父母與祖母被要求前往奉化方橋派出所；聲援者的現場記錄及海外報道則記下警方控制聚集、勸離及送返聲援者的情況。
  5. 2026年6月15日至17日： 家屬影片、銳報和新唐人報道記錄父母與祖母被要求前往方橋派出所並留在所內要求解決問題；上述海外報道另記錄一名赴寧波聲援者遭警方攔截、扣留和強制送返。

Incident overview

"Xiao Luoxi" is a five-month-old baby girl. Ningbo official notification stated that she died after surgery at the Women and Children's Hospital Affiliated to Ningbo University on November 14, 2025, and was recognized as a Grade I Class A medical accident in February 2026.

The family members continue to pursue the diagnosis and treatment, medical records, surgical procedures and responsibilities through litigation, public videos and in-person advocacy; these materials from those directly affected, on-site support records and overseas dissemination together preserve the complete context of the incident.

Detailed timeline

  1. November 14, 2025: Little Luoxi died after surgery at the Women and Children’s Hospital of Ningbo University; her family members subsequently continued to demand medical records, surgical instructions and responsibility responses.
  2. January 12, 2026: Officially, the relevant experts recused themselves, and nine experts formed an appraisal team under the supervision of notarization and representatives of the local People’s Congress and the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference.
  3. 5 February 2026: Ningbo classified the case as a Grade I, Class A medical accident, assigned primary responsibility to the medical provider, and acknowledged failures in clinical quality and safety, risk prevention, emergency response, and doctor-patient communication. The notice said the lead surgeon’s licence was revoked, the hospital director and deputy director were removed, and police had opened an investigation.
  4. June 2026: The family continued to seek answers through videos and in-person petitions. Family material said the parents and grandmother were required to go to Fangqiao police station in Fenghua; supporters’ records and overseas reports documented police control of the gathering and the removal or return of supporters.
  5. 15–17 June 2026: Family videos and reporting by Ruibao and NTD recorded that the parents and grandmother were required to attend Fangqiao police station and remained there seeking a resolution; the overseas reports also recorded that police intercepted, held and forcibly returned a supporter travelling to Ningbo.

Key evidence and information control

The family’s lawsuit materials, videos and records of in-person petitions preserve the death of the five-month-old infant, questions about the medical file and surgery, and the family’s account that they were required to go to Fangqiao police station. Public versions should protect the infant’s and relatives’ privacy.

The medical-accident notice, overseas reporting by Ruibao and NTD, and supporters’ field records separately document the Class I Grade A determination, hospital-management failures, restrictions on supporters, deleted posts, reduced visibility and account limits.

Authorities found the hospital primarily responsible and sanctioned hospital personnel. The original medical records, surgical decisions, full causal analysis, the family’s treatment at Fangqiao police station, the legal basis for restricting supporters, and effective legal and administrative remedies still require disclosure and independent review.

Pressure on advocacy and state intervention

The Grade I, Class A finding and the acknowledgement of hospital-management failures establish an important part of medical responsibility, but they do not answer questions about the original records, operative decisions, causation analysis, police contact, full compensation, or judicial redress.

When the family continued to seek answers, police-station intervention, restrictions on supporters, and pressure on online circulation drew a dispute that should have been handled through transparent medical review, administrative accountability, and the courts into a stability-control framework, further narrowing the space for safe speech and evidence preservation.

Human rights and rule-of-law analysis

Five-month-old girl Xiao Luoxi died after surgery at the Women and Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Ningbo University; even after the case was classified as a Grade I Class A medical accident, her family still lacked a transparent account of the records, operative decisions, and chain of responsibility. When the family pursued litigation, videos, and public statements, the Fangqiao Police Station episode, restrictions on in-person solidarity, removal of online posts, and obstacles to preserving evidence added a second injury of police-led stability control and information suppression to the unresolved medical harm. The hospital, health authorities, and police must disclose verifiable records, accept an independent investigation, stop suppressing the family and supporters, and secure access to litigation, peaceful expression, evidence preservation, and an effective remedy.

Relevance to protection review

The family’s publicly documented role in litigation, its public videos, the public record that family members were required to attend Fangqiao Police Station, supporters’ activities, posts deleted or made less visible, and the digital trail left by overseas preservation and circulation allow police and platforms to continue identifying relatives, supporters, and people who share the case. Police involvement and restrictions on solidarity turned a medical complaint into a second injury, while platform controls narrowed the space for preserving evidence and seeking public support. China lacks a safe complaint channel, independent medical review, and a judicial remedy free from interference; the Chinese government must stop reprisals and digital tracking, protect family contact and public expression, and ensure that continued documentation or return to China does not expose participants to police pressure.
