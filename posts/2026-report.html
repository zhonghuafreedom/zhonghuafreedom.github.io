這份更新把網站時間線延伸至2025–2026年。公開資料顯示，中國人權問題不再只是個別事件，而是一套由資訊控制、國安化治理、數字監控、司法恐嚇和跨境壓力共同構成的日常控制系統。

一、國安化治理成為通用工具

「國家安全」「尋釁滋事」「煽動顛覆」「境外勢力」等模糊概念，繼續被用來處理記者、律師、影像創作者、勞工倡議者、宗教人士、女權與LGBTI相關表達。這種做法的核心不是單一案件，而是讓普通人難以預測何種言論會被刑事化。

二、公民社會從公開空間退入低可見度生存

公益律師、勞工NGO、女權小組、獨立媒體與公共知識分子仍面對長期監控、限制出境、指定居所監視居住、吊銷執照與社交媒體封禁。許多非政治議題只要觸及政府責任，也可能被視為「不穩定因素」。

三、社會抗議仍在發生，而且更加分散

欠薪、爛尾樓、農村土地、地方財政壓力、校園暴力和養老金等問題引發的集體行動，在2025年顯著增加。這些行動多是地方性和權益型，但反映出經濟壓力與治理失靈正在累積。

四、香港、新疆、西藏與海外華人社群互相連動

香港國安體制繼續擴張；新疆與西藏的強制同化、宗教控制與勞動轉移問題仍被聯合國專家和人權組織追蹤；海外港人、維吾爾人、藏人和中國異議人士則面對騷擾、懸賞、法律戰和家屬施壓。

This update extends the site timeline into 2025–2026. Public reporting shows that the human rights crisis in China is not a set of isolated incidents, but a routine system combining information control, national-security governance, digital surveillance, judicial intimidation, and overseas pressure.

1. National-security governance as a general-purpose tool

Vague labels such as “national security,” “picking quarrels,” “inciting subversion,” and “foreign forces” continue to be applied to journalists, lawyers, filmmakers, labour advocates, religious communities, feminist and LGBTI expression. The effect is broader than any single case: ordinary people cannot reliably know which speech or association will be criminalized.

2. Civil society pushed into low-visibility survival

Public-interest lawyers, labour NGOs, feminist groups, independent media, and public intellectuals face surveillance, exit bans, residential surveillance at a designated location, loss of licenses, and social-media bans. Even issues that appear nonpolitical can become risky when they touch government responsibility.

3. Social protest persists and is more dispersed

Wage arrears, unfinished housing, rural land seizures, local fiscal pressure, school violence, pensions, and workplace grievances triggered many local actions in 2025. Most remain rights-based and local, but together they reveal mounting economic pressure and governance failure.

4. Hong Kong, Xinjiang, Tibet, and diaspora communities are connected

Hong Kong’s national-security architecture continues to expand. Forced assimilation, religious control, and labour-transfer concerns in Xinjiang and Tibet remain under scrutiny. Overseas Hongkongers, Uyghurs, Tibetans, and Chinese dissidents face harassment, bounties, lawfare, and pressure on family members in China.
